## Method

- **Simulated**: hour-mean dry PM2.5 that the model attributes to directly emitted sources (EDGAR v8.1 sectors in India; an ESTIMATED road and construction dust source in India, spread from TERI 2016 national totals; satellite-detected fires anywhere in the model domain, 66-98°E, 6-38°N, including Pakistan, Nepal and Bangladesh), the value the app shows for a city: the mean over the 3 x 3 block of 0.1 degree cells around it (about 30 km), summed over all source tags at default weights (every weight 1, fire scaling 1, fires continue). An hour is missing if any tag is missing there.
- **Measured**: CPCB PM2.5 via OpenAQ, the median over up to 5 stations within 25 km. CPCB hour averages run on the Indian clock (HH:30 UTC); each UTC hour is the overlap-weighted mean of the two periods it straddles, missing if either is missing. Nothing is interpolated.
- **CAMS**: Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service PM2.5 from the Open-Meteo air-quality API at the city point, shown as a reference model that does include secondary aerosol and transport from outside India. CAMS values are instantaneous at HH:00 UTC, while model and CPCB values are means over [HH:00, HH+1:00) UTC: about a 30-minute offset.
- **Metrics** over hours where both series exist: n, Pearson r, mean bias (simulated minus measured, µg/m³), NMB = Σ(sim − obs) / Σ obs, RMSE (µg/m³), FAC2 = share of hours with 0.5 ≤ sim/obs ≤ 2 (obs > 0). Fields are "–" when n < 24. Mean measured and mean simulated are over the same paired hours. CAMS is compared over the SAME hours as the model (hours where simulated, measured and CAMS values all exist), so the two rows are comparable. Pooled rows concatenate the cities' paired hours: pooled NMB is a ratio of sums, and pooled r is a correlation across all city-hours, which includes differences between cities, so it is not hour-to-hour agreement; the median per-city r describes that.
- **Day and night**: hours (UTC [t, t+1)) starting 10:00-17:59 IST are day, 22:00-05:59 IST night; other hours are in neither.
- **Hours and runs**: every UTC hour of the episode is used once. Whole 6-day blocks come from the run whose 144-hour window starts on the block's first day (run date = block start + 2 days); a shorter final block comes from the run whose window ends on the episode's last day. Each run includes 48 hours of spin-up before its window.
- **Not simulated**: secondary aerosol (nitrate, sulphate, secondary organics), anthropogenic inventory emissions outside India (vehicles, industry, residential, for example in Pakistani Punjab; fires there ARE simulated and appear under "other states and neighbouring countries", with the generic vegetation emission factor, not the crop-residue one, a known approximation for crop-residue fires in Pakistani Punjab), road and construction dust (no gridded inventory), vertical structure (one mixed layer), chemistry; emissions are annual means with no daily or seasonal cycle. Low readings are expected from these omissions. Possible causes of other errors include errors in transport, mixing height or the placement of emissions; this report does not separate them.
